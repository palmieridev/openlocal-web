EOQ ("economic order quantity") answers one very concrete question: **how big should each purchase be?**

Ordering little and often costs you in freight, time and trips. Ordering a lot at once ties up cash and risks stock going off. EOQ is the middle point between those two costs.

## The numbers it uses

- **Demand**: how much of that variant you sell in a period. It comes from your stock-out movements.
- **Order cost**: what it costs to place one order regardless of size (freight, travel, time).
- **Holding cost**: what it costs to keep one unit for the period (space, capital, expiry).

Demand includes **sale** movements within the selected period. Purchases, production, adjustments and losses do not count as demand.

## Try it with the demo data

1. Open **Analytics → EOQ**.
2. Search for SKU `TR-BUFANDA-AZUL`. The calculator will find the **Azul marino** variant of *Bufanda de telar*.
3. Use a **90-day** period, an order cost of **$100**, and a holding cost of **20%**.
4. Select **Calculate EOQ**.

With the seeded movements, recorded demand is **3 units** and the result is an EOQ of **55 units**.

<Callout tone="warning" title="This is an example, not a purchase recommendation">
The result is high compared with demand because the example's assumed costs carry a lot of weight in the calculation. Replace $100 and 20% with your actual costs before making a decision.
</Callout>

## Reading the result

The output is an order size, not a purchase order. If EOQ suggests 40 units and your supplier sells cases of 24, order 48 or 24 — the number is a guide, not a rule.

A very large EOQ almost always means the order cost is overstated. A very small one means the holding cost came out too high.

<Callout tone="note" title="It assumes demand like the demand you recorded">
With only a few weeks of movements, treat the result as a first approximation and revisit it once you have more history.
</Callout>

## How often to revisit

When supplier prices change, when your sales rhythm shifts noticeably, or at least once a season.
