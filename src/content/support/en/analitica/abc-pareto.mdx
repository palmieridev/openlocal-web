ABC/Pareto analysis ranks your products by the value of recorded sales. It helps you decide where to focus inventory cash, attention and space first.

## What each column means

- **Value**: the product's sales amount during the period analyzed.
- **Cumulative %**: how much of total value comes from that product and all the products above it.
- **Class A**: products that account for most of the value. Protect them from stockouts and review them more often.
- **Class B**: products of intermediate importance. Keep an eye on them without giving them the same attention as class A.
- **Class C**: the rest of the catalog. You can review them less frequently, but the class does not mean you should discontinue them.

The sweater lands in A because it generates nearly three quarters of the value on its own. The scarf takes the cumulative share to 92% and lands in B; the coin purse completes the total and lands in C.

Products with no recorded sales appear with $0 value in class C. That does not prove they are poor products: it only means they have not contributed value to the history being analyzed yet.

## Which movements count

The report adds only stock-out movements recorded as **sales**. Purchases, production, adjustments and losses change inventory, but they do not contribute to sales value.

<Callout tone="warning" title="ABC looks backward">
The classification describes recorded sales; it does not forecast demand or measure profit margin. A product in class A by sales may not be the most profitable one.
</Callout>

## Turn each class into an action

1. Check availability and reorder points for class A products first.
2. Watch whether a class B product is growing before shifting more budget toward it.
3. Review class C periodically to separate new or seasonal products from items you should actually scale back.
4. Run the analysis again after a season or a major sales change.
